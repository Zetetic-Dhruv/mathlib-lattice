# Statement specification

Every statement the project proves, organized by module. Each module lists its definitions
and its theorems, marked **[P]** (general principle), **[S]** (specific consequence or
instance of a principle), or **[C]** (converse or characterization: the specific facts that
lead back up to, or are equivalent to, a principle). The Lean skeleton states all of these
with `sorry` proofs; definitions are real definitions, never `sorry`.

Orientation conventions, used everywhere:
- Mathlib's `Concept α β r` is ordered by extent inclusion. A concept with a larger extent is
  more general. Objects (`α`) are statements (declarations); attributes (`β`) are
  descriptions (patterns, schemas, transformations).
- The specificity (instantiation) order on terms: `s ≤ t` iff `s` is an instance of `t`.
  Specific is below general; least general generalization (lgg) is the join.
- Pattern structures use Ganter–Kuznetsov's information order on descriptions `D`:
  `d ≤ e` iff `e` carries at least the information of `d`; the meet is similarity (lgg).
  This is the order dual of the specificity order.
- Hypothesis lattices for version spaces: `h ≤ h'` iff `h'` is more general; the extension
  map `ext` is monotone.

## Order/Concept/Density.lean — the basic theorem (representation half)
Mathlib anchors: `Concept`, `Concept.ofObject`, `Concept.ofAttribute`, `Concept.ofObjects`,
`Concept.ofAttributes`, `Concept.ofObject_le_ofAttribute_iff`, `Concept.extent_iSup`,
`Concept.intent_iInf`.

Definitions
- `SupDense (γ : ι → L)` for a complete lattice `L`: every `x` is `⨆` of the `γ i ≤ x`.
- `InfDense (μ : κ → L)`: every `x` is `⨅` of the `μ j ≥ x`.

Theorems
- [P] Object concepts are join-dense: `SupDense (Concept.ofObject r)`.
- [P] Attribute concepts are meet-dense: `InfDense (Concept.ofAttribute r)`.
- [S] `c = ⨆ a ∈ c.extent, Concept.ofObject r a`, and dually `c = ⨅ b ∈ c.intent, ofAttribute r b`.
- [P] Representation: given `γ : α → L` join-dense, `μ : β → L` meet-dense and
  `r a b ↔ γ a ≤ μ b`, the map `x ↦ ⟨{a | γ a ≤ x}, {b | x ≤ μ b}⟩` is an order isomorphism
  `L ≃o Concept α β r` (define it, with an `apply` lemma for the extent).
- [C] `Nonempty (L ≃o Concept α β r) ↔ ∃ γ μ, SupDense γ ∧ InfDense μ ∧ ∀ a b, r a b ↔ γ a ≤ μ b`.
- [S] Standard context: `L ≃o Concept L L (· ≤ ·)` for every complete lattice `L`.
- [S] Density transports along order isomorphisms.

## Order/Concept/Reduced.lean — irreducibles, reduced contexts, normal forms
Mathlib anchors: `SupIrred`, `InfIrred`, `exists_supIrred_decomposition`,
`exists_infIrred_decomposition`.

Definitions
- Completely sup-irreducible: `x ≠ ⨆ y ∈ Set.Iio x, y` (and the inf dual).
- The context of irreducibles of `L`: objects `{x // SupIrred x}`, attributes
  `{x // InfIrred x}`, incidence `j.1 ≤ m.1`.
- Clarified and reduced contexts (Ganter–Wille, Def. 23–24), stated for finite contexts.

Theorems
- [P] Every completely sup-irreducible element lies in the range of every join-dense map
  (no finiteness). Dual for meet-dense maps.
- [S] In a finite lattice, `SupIrred x` ↔ completely sup-irreducible; hence every
  join-dense family contains all sup-irreducibles.
- [P] If the completely sup-irreducibles are join-dense and the completely inf-irreducibles
  are meet-dense (doubly founded lattices), `L` is isomorphic to the concept lattice of its
  context of irreducibles.
- [S] Every finite lattice is isomorphic to the concept lattice of its irreducibles.
- [P] Uniqueness: a finite reduced context is isomorphic, compatibly with incidence, to the
  context of irreducibles of its concept lattice.
- [S] Normal form: in a finite lattice, `x = ⨅ {m | InfIrred m ∧ x ≤ m}`; the map
  `x ↦ {m | InfIrred m ∧ x ≤ m}` is an order embedding into `(Set {m // InfIrred m})ᵒᵈ`.
- [C] A finite lattice is distributive iff every element has a unique irredundant
  decomposition as a join of sup-irreducibles.

## Order/Concept/Implication.lean — implications and the Duquenne–Guigues base
Definitions (attributes `β`)
- `Implication β` (premise and conclusion, sets of attributes).
- Holds in a context `r`: every object having the premise has the conclusion.
- Respect, the closure of a set under a set of implications (as a `ClosureOperator`),
  entailment, completeness of an implication set for `r`.
- Pseudo-intent (recursive: `P ≠ P''` and `Q'' ⊆ P` for every pseudo-intent `Q ⊊ P`;
  define by well-founded recursion on cardinality for finite `β`).
- `dgBase r`: the implications `P → P''` for pseudo-intents `P`.

Theorems
- [S] An implication holds iff its conclusion lies in the intent closure of its premise.
- [P] The closure generated by `dgBase r` is the intent closure of `r` (finite `β`).
- [S] `dgBase r` is complete (sound and generates every valid implication).
- [S] `dgBase r` is irredundant.
- [P] Minimality: every complete implication set has at least as many implications as
  `dgBase r` (Guigues–Duquenne 1986).
- [C] The key lemma: every complete implication set contains, for each pseudo-intent `P`,
  an implication whose premise generates `P`.
- [P] For finite `β`, closure operators on `Set β`, implication theories, and intent
  families of contexts present the same objects (each closure system is the closed-set
  family of an implication set and the intent family of a context).

## Order/Concept/Exploration.lean — attribute exploration with an oracle
Model: a universe context `R : Ω → β → Prop` (all possible objects, unknown), finite `β`,
known objects `E ⊆ Ω`. An oracle answers an implication either by "holds in `R`" or by an
object of `Ω` violating it.

Definitions
- The exploration procedure (lectic order / next-closure over attribute sets), defined
  relationally or by well-founded recursion; its states and runs.

Theorems
- [P] Existence of a finite example basis: there is a finite `E ⊆ Ω` with at most as many
  objects as `R` has intents, whose intent closure equals that of `R`.
- [P] Termination: every run with a correct oracle terminates.
- [P] Correctness: at termination the accepted implications are `dgBase R` and the known
  objects have the same intent closure as `R`.
- [S] Soundness of each step: accepted implications hold in `R`; each counterexample violates
  the implication it answers.

## Order/Concept/Incremental.lean — adding objects (corpus history)
Theorems (context `r` extended by one new object with attribute set `S`)
- [P] Every intent of `r` is an intent of the extended context.
- [C] The intents of the extended context are the old intents together with the sets
  `B ∩ S` for old intents `B`.
- [S] The number of concepts at most doubles.
- [S] Along an increasing sequence of object sets, the intent families increase.

## Order/PatternStructure/Basic.lean — pattern structures (Ganter–Kuznetsov 2001)
Setting: `δ : G → D`, `D` a complete meet-semilattice (`CompleteSemilatticeInf`).

Definitions
- `extentOf δ d = {g | d ≤ δ g}`, `descr δ A = ⨅ g ∈ A, δ g`, pattern concepts
  (`extentOf δ (descr δ A) = A`), their complete lattice.

Theorems
- [P] `A ⊆ extentOf δ d ↔ d ≤ descr δ A` (an antitone Galois connection).
- [S] Pattern concepts form a complete lattice.
- [P] Representation: pattern concepts ≃o `Concept G D (fun g d => d ≤ δ g)`.
- [S] Formal contexts are pattern structures (descriptions `(Set β)` under `⊆` as
  information order, meet `∩`): their pattern concepts ≃o `Concept α β r`.

## Order/PatternStructure/Projection.lean — projections (height truncation)
Definitions
- Projection: `ψ : D → D` monotone, `ψ d ≤ d`, idempotent.

Theorems
- [P] Every extent of the projected structure `ψ ∘ δ` is an extent of `δ` (no
  meet-preservation needed).
- [S] If `ψ ∘ ψ' = ψ`, every `ψ`-extent is a `ψ'`-extent.
- [S] For a chain of projections `ψ k` with `ψ k ∘ ψ (k+1) = ψ k`, the extent families
  increase with `k`.
- [P] Mapping a coarse pattern concept to the fine one with the same extent is an order
  embedding with a left adjoint (coarse closure): a Galois insertion.

## Order/Mobius/Cumulative.lean — cumulative counts and point counts
Mathlib anchors: `IncidenceAlgebra.mu`, `IncidenceAlgebra.moebius_inversion_bot`,
`IncidenceAlgebra.zeta`.

Setting: a locally finite partial order `P` with `⊥`, a finite type `X`, `f : X → P`,
weights `w : X → R`.

Definitions
- `cum f w p = ∑ x with f x ≤ p, w x`, `pt f w p = ∑ x with f x = p, w x`.

Theorems
- [P] `pt f w p = ∑ q ≤ p, μ q p * cum f w q` (the Möbius inverse of the cumulative count is
  the point count), and `cum f w p = ∑ q ≤ p, pt f w q`.
- [P] Pull-back along an upper adjoint: for `GaloisConnection l u`,
  `cum (l ∘ f) w q = cum f w (u q)`.
- [S] Push-forward along any map: `pt (l ∘ f) w q = ∑ p with l p = q, pt f w p`.
- [C] A function has a nonnegative Möbius inverse iff it is the cumulative count of a
  nonnegative weighting.

## Order/Mobius/Rota.lean — Rota's Galois connection theorem and corollaries
Theorems
- [P] Rota: for `GaloisConnection l u` between locally finite orders, `a : P`, `b : Q`:
  `∑ x ∈ Icc a (u b) with l x = b, μ a x = ∑ y ∈ Icc (l a) b with u y = a, μ y b`.
- [S] Closure form: for a closure operator, the sum of `μ a x` over `x` with closure `b`
  is `μ_closed a b` when `a` is closed and `0` otherwise.
- [S] Boolean Möbius function: on `Finset α`, `μ s t = (-1) ^ (t \ s).card` for `s ⊆ t`.
- [S] Cross-cut theorem: in a finite lattice, `μ ⊥ b = ∑ (-1)^|S|` over sets `S` of atoms
  below `b` with `sSup S = b`.
- [S] Weisner: in a finite lattice, for `a ≠ ⊥`, `∑ x with x ⊔ a = b, μ ⊥ x = 0`.
- [S] Distributive lattices: `μ x y = (-1)^k` if `[x, y]` is Boolean with `k` atoms, else `0`.

## Order/Mobius/Chain.lean — chains
- [P] In a locally finite linear order, `μ i j` is `1` if `i = j`, `-1` if `i ⋖ j`, `0`
  otherwise.
- [S] On `ℕ`: Möbius inversion is first differences.

## Order/Belief/Basic.lean — belief functions on lattices (Grabisch 2009)
Definitions
- Mass (Möbius inverse) of `f : L → ℝ` on a finite lattice; belief function: nonnegative
  mass and `f ⊤ = 1`.
- Coverage of a finite context: `c ↦ |c.extent| / |α|`; mass of a concept: the share of
  objects whose object concept is `c`.
- Unstated concept: a concept that is not an object concept.

Theorems
- [S] Coverage is a belief function; its mass at `c` is `|{a | ofObject r a = c}| / |α|`
  (instance of the cumulative/point principle with `f = ofObject`).
- [S] `mass c > 0 ↔ c` is an object concept.
- [S] An unstated concept is the join of the object concepts strictly below it.
- [S] The same for pattern concepts.
- [S] Masses push forward along coarsening of description languages.
- [P] On a finite distributive lattice, nonnegative mass is equivalent to total
  monotonicity; [C] this equivalence fails for some non-distributive lattice
  (counterexample).

## VersionSpace/Basic.lean — version spaces in a hypothesis lattice
Setting: complete lattice `H`, monotone `ext : H → Set X`, positives `P`, negatives `N`.

Definitions
- `versionSpace ext P N = {h | P ⊆ ext h ∧ Disjoint (ext h) N}`.
- Join gap: `ext (h ⊔ h') \ (ext h ∪ ext h')`.
- Certain predictions `⋂ h ∈ V, ext h`; possible predictions `⋃ h ∈ V, ext h`.

Theorems
- [P] Version spaces are order-convex (`Set.OrdConnected`).
- [S] Positive constraints form an upper set; negative constraints form a lower set.
- [S] If `ext` preserves binary meets, version spaces are closed under meets; if it
  preserves arbitrary meets, a nonempty version space has a least element.
- [S] For `h h'` in a version space, `h ⊔ h'` is in it iff the join gap misses `N`.
- [C] Every version space is closed under joins iff every join gap is empty
  (equivalently `ext` preserves binary joins).
- [S] Maximal elements form an antichain; in a well-founded `H`, every element of the
  version space lies below a maximal one.
- [S] With a least element `S`: certain predictions are `ext S`; possible predictions are
  the union over maximal elements.
- [S] Version spaces shrink as evidence grows.

## VersionSpace/Concept.lean — version spaces of samples are concept extents
Setting: hypotheses `Θ`, examples `E`, consistency `cons : Θ → E → Prop`.
- [S] The version space of a sample `S` is `lowerPolar cons S`, the intersection of the
  single-example version spaces.
- [S] The reachable version spaces are exactly the extents of the context `(Θ, E, cons)`.
- [S] Their number is the number of formal concepts.
- [P] Bridge: for a statement context `r` with positives `Q` and negatives `N`, the
  consistent concepts form an order-convex set with least element `ofObjects r Q` when
  nonempty.

## VersionSpace/Learner.lean — the learner that returns the first consistent hypothesis
Setting: `hs : List Θ`, decidable `cons`, samples as lists of examples,
`firstConsistent hs w = hs.find? (consistent with all of w)`.

Definitions
- Output automata over an alphabet with a finite state type; computing a function of words.
- Nerode equivalence of a function of words.

Theorems
- [P] Myhill–Nerode for output functions: the least number of states of an automaton
  computing `F` is the number of Nerode classes of `F` (when finite).
- [S] For `F = firstConsistent hs` over all samples, the Nerode classes correspond to
  reachable version spaces, so the least number of states is their number.
- [P] Mind changes along any example sequence are at most the number of extensionally
  distinct hypotheses in `hs`.
- [P] Identification in the limit: along an infinite sequence consistent with some
  hypothesis of `hs`, the output is eventually constant and consistent.
- [S] On a list sorted by size, the output is a consistent hypothesis of least size; it is
  the maximum a posteriori hypothesis under any prior strictly decreasing along the list.
- [S] Extensionally equal hypotheses lie in the same reachable version spaces.

## Height/Filtration.lean — the height filtration
Setting: pattern structure `δ`, chain of projections `ψ : ℕ → (D → D)` with
`ψ k ∘ ψ (k+1) = ψ k`.
- [S] Extents increase with `k`; extent closures decrease with `k`.
- [S] Join gaps are antitone in `k`.
- [S] The level map is a Galois insertion; masses push forward, cumulative counts pull back.
- [S] Möbius functions of consecutive levels are related by Rota's theorem.
- [S] The number of concepts is monotone in `k`.

## Term/FirstOrder.lean — first-order terms (Plotkin–Reynolds)
Mathlib anchors: `FirstOrder.Language.Term`, `FirstOrder.Language.Term.subst`.

Definitions
- Instantiation preorder on `L.Term α` (`s ≤ t` iff `t.subst σ = s` for some `σ`), on a type
  synonym; renaming equivalence; the anti-unification algorithm `lgg` (a real definition).

Theorems
- [P] Instantiation is a preorder; mutual instances are renamings of each other.
- [P] `lgg s t` is a least upper bound of `s, t` (needs infinitely many variables).
- [P] Two terms with a common instance have a most general common instance (unification).
- [P] Every nonempty set of terms has a least general generalization; every set with a
  common instance has a most general common instance (complete lattice after quotienting by
  renaming and adjoining `⊥`).
- [S] Every term has finitely many generalizations up to renaming (intervals are finite).

## Term/Pattern.lean — higher-order patterns (Miller 1991, Pfenning 1991)
Definitions
- λ-terms with de Bruijn indices, constants and metavariables applied to bound variables;
  the pattern condition (distinct bound-variable arguments); metavariable substitution with
  normalization; instantiation preorder.

Theorems
- [P] Patterns have unique least general generalizations up to renaming.
- [P] Unifiable patterns have most general unifiers, unique up to renaming.
- [C] Outside the pattern fragment both fail: an explicit pair of terms with two
  incomparable maximal common instances.

## Term/PatternStructure.lean — terms as descriptions
- [S] Terms with lgg as meet (information order) form a complete meet-semilattice after
  quotienting by renaming and adjoining a top element.
- [S] For a finite corpus, the pattern concept lattice is isomorphic to the concept lattice
  of `(corpus, descriptions, instance-of)`.
- [S] The intents of the pattern concepts are exactly the lggs of nonempty subsets of the
  corpus.

## Term/Hypothesis.lean — generalization with hypotheses (θ-subsumption)
Definitions
- Clauses (finite sets of literals over first-order atoms), θ-subsumption preorder;
  relative subsumption with respect to background Horn clauses (typeclass hierarchy).

Theorems
- [P] θ-subsumption is a preorder; clauses have least general generalizations (Plotkin
  1970).
- [P] Relative least general generalizations exist for finite ground background theories
  (Plotkin 1971).
- [S] Weakening hypotheses is a move up the subsumption order.

## Transform/Basic.lean — transformations as second-level hypotheses
Setting: statements `X`, a type `T` acting by `apply : T → X → X`, example pairs `X × X`.
- [S] The version space of transformations from pairs is `lowerPolar` of the pairs for the
  relation `apply t p.1 = p.2`, so the learner results apply.
- [P] Composition: consistent `t₁` (pairs `P₁`) and `t₂` (pairs `P₂`) compose to a hypothesis
  consistent with the relational composite of `P₁` and `P₂`.
- [C] Version spaces of composites contain the composites of version spaces; the inclusion
  can be strict (counterexample).

## Transform/Dictionary.lean — language maps
Mathlib anchors: `FirstOrder.Language.LHom`, `LHom.onTerm`, `LHom.onSentence`,
`LHom.realize_onSentence`.
- [P] Language maps commute with substitution, hence are monotone for instantiation.
- [P] Injective language maps preserve least general generalizations up to renaming.
- [C] A language map is consistent with translation pairs iff it agrees with the symbol
  correspondence forced by aligning the pairs; that correspondence is well defined iff the
  pairs align.
- [S] Translations along language maps preserve truth in expansions (Mathlib).
- [S] Order duality as a language map on the language of lattices, sound for `OrderDual`.

## Transform/Action.lean — transformation groups acting on the lattice
- [P] A group acting on statements by automorphisms of the instantiation order acts on the
  concept lattice of any invariant context by order automorphisms.
- [S] Masses are invariant under the action.
- [S] The number of orbits of concepts is the average number of fixed concepts (Burnside).

## Transform/Capacity.lean — capacity of two-level classes
- [P] The number of behaviors on a sample of a class of composites is at most the number of
  behaviors of the inner class times the maximum number of behaviors of the outer class on
  an image.
- [S] Growth function of composites at most the product of growth functions.

## Transform/Galois.lean — Galois transport
- [S] Definition: a statement is a Galois-transport instance of a generic statement at a
  Galois connection; soundness follows from the generic lemma (citation).
- [S] The declarations derived from one Galois connection form the extent of the concept
  whose intent is that connection's generic statements.

## Query/Basic.lean — the generalization query
Setting: finite context `r : α → β → Prop` (statements including refuted ones,
descriptions), positives `Q : Set α`, negatives `N : Set α`.

Definitions
- Consistent concepts, least consistent concept `ofObjects r Q`, maximal consistent
  concepts, certain and possible predictions, coverage, mass, unstated concepts, normal form
  (inf-irreducibles above).

Theorems
- [S] Consistent concepts exist iff `(ofObjects r Q).extent` misses `N`.
- [S] When they exist, `ofObjects r Q` is the least consistent concept.
- [S] The consistent concepts are order-convex; the maximal ones form an antichain and
  every consistent concept lies below one.
- [S] Certain predictions are `(ofObjects r Q).extent`; possible predictions are the union of
  the maximal extents.
- [S] More positives or more negatives shrink the consistent set.
- [S] A consistent concept is unstated iff its mass is zero iff it is a proper join.
- [S] Every answer has a unique normal form.
- [S] At a finer description level the least consistent concept's extent shrinks.
- [S] Adding a declaration changes the answer as described by the incremental theorems.
